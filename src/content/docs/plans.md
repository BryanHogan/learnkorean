This is the private writing roadmap for Stage I of the course. Chapters should stay
short and focused, at roughly the same size as the existing Unit 0 chapters.

**Unit 0 status labels**

- **Existing — light revision:** The chapter is usable but needs a small, focused update.
- **Existing — revise:** The chapter needs a meaningful correction or expansion.
- **New:** The chapter still needs to be written.

## Unit 0 — Read Hangul

The goal of Unit 0 is to teach learners to decode Hangul without relying on
romanisation and to recognise the most important pronunciation changes. Sound
changes are introduced here, then reinforced throughout the course.

1. **The Korean Alphabet and Syllable Blocks**.
2. **Aspirated Consonants**
3. **Tense (Double) Consonants** (tense consonants)
4. **Compound Vowels** — distinguish compound vowels from true diphthongs
5. **Batchim and Final Sounds** — **New.** Cover final-consonant placement, the seven
   realised final sounds, final-sound neutralisation, and basic recognition of
   double 받침.
6. **Essential Sound Changes** — **New.** Introduce liaison, nasalisation,
   liquidisation, aspiration, tensification, and palatalisation for recognition
   rather than complete mastery.
7. **Basic Korean Words and Expressions** — **Existing — light revision.** Clarify
   vocabulary labels such as `저` and `나`, add useful context, and remove the claim
   that knowing the letters alone lets learners pronounce every Korean word.
8. **Hangul Practice and Next Steps** — **Existing — revise.** Update the completion
   page with internal links to the new pronunciation chapters, handwriting and
   keyboard practice, unfamiliar-word reading, and a romanisation exit check.

## Unit 1 — Build First Sentences

The goal of Unit 1 is to let learners exchange greetings, identify people and
things, and produce a short polite self-introduction.

1. Korean sentence structure
2. Greetings and goodbye (Brief mention of 요, `존댓말`/`반말` distinction)
3. Is and is not (+ self-introduction, maybe separate chapter)
4. Verb introduction
5. Present tense conjugation
6. Object marker
7. Topic marker
8. This and that
9. questions, why, what, how, where
10. Self-introduction
11. Action verbs
12. Descriptive verbs
13. Adverbs

Do not create separate unit-introduction pages. The first substantive chapter in
each unit should briefly introduce that unit’s goals.

## Unit 4 — Particles and Places

The goal of Unit 4 is to let learners describe where people and objects are,
where actions happen, and how people or things move between places.

1. **The Object Particle 을/를 and Natural Omission** — Teach object marking and
   common conversational omission.
2. **Location and Destination: 에 and 에서** — Contrast static location or
   destination with the place where an action occurs.
3. **Possession, Inclusion, and Restriction: 의, 도, and 만** — Teach these
   common noun relationships in context.
4. **Lists and Accompaniment: 와/과, 하고, and (이)랑** — Compare their normal
   written and spoken uses.
5. **Recipients and Sources: 에게, 한테, 께, and Their Source Forms** — Cover
   recipients, politeness differences, `에게서`, and `한테서`.
6. **Direction and Means with -(으)로** — Introduce direction, route, means, and
   method.
7. **From, To, and Until: 부터, 에서, and 까지** — Teach spatial and temporal
   ranges.
8. **Describing Places** — Revisit `은/는` and `이/가` while describing a home,
   school, workplace, or neighbourhood.

## Unit 5 — Questions, Requests, and Conversation Repair

The goal of Unit 5 is to help learners get information, make polite requests, and
recover when they do not understand a conversation.

1. **Core Question Words** — Cover what, who, where, when, why, and how in complete
   beginner sentences.
2. **Choosing Between Things** — Explain the differences between `무슨`, `어떤`,
   and `어느`.
3. **Simple Requests and Instructions** — Teach `주세요` and `-(으)세요` in
   common service and classroom situations.
4. **Asking Favors and Giving Negative Commands** — Teach `-아/어 주세요` and
   `-지 마세요`.
5. **Getting Attention and Repairing a Conversation** — Cover repetition, slower
   speech, clarification, understanding, and confirmation.
6. **At a Café** — Combine questions and requests in a practical ordering exchange.

## Unit 6 — Numbers, Dates, and Schedules

The goal of Unit 6 is to let learners handle time, dates, prices, quantities, and
simple appointments.

1. **Sino-Korean Numbers** — Teach number formation and the situations that use
   the Sino-Korean system.
2. **Native Korean Numbers and Their Short Forms** — Teach native numbers and
   `한`, `두`, `세`, `네`, and `스무`.
3. **Counters and Asking How Many** — Introduce common counters and questions with
   `몇`.
4. **Telling Time and Expressing Duration** — Combine native hours, Sino-Korean
   minutes and seconds, and basic duration expressions.
5. **Dates, Days, Years, and Age** — Cover days of the week, calendar dates, years,
   and the common ways to state age.
6. **Prices and Korean Currency** — Practise prices, questions with `얼마`, and a
   basic shopping exchange.
7. **Phone Numbers, Addresses, and Digit Strings** — Teach practical conventions
   for reading identifying numbers.
8. **Schedules and Appointments** — Combine frequency, approximation with
   `얼마나` and related expressions, calendar language, and arranging a meeting.

## Writing order

1. Add Unit 0’s batchim and sound-change chapters before treating that unit as
   complete.
2. Correct the existing aspirated-consonant, tense-consonant, and compound-vowel
   explanations.
3. Revise the two existing Unit 1 drafts, then complete Unit 1.
4. Write Units 2 and 3 to establish the essential tense system.
5. Write Units 4 through 6 to complete the Stage I beginner milestone.

Only create a separate milestone or review page when it teaches or assesses enough
material to justify a full chapter. Otherwise, include the checkpoint in the last
substantive chapter of the unit.
