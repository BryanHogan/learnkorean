---
title: "반말: Casual speech in Korean"
description: How to use 반말 in Korean, using the casual speech for speaking with close friends.
---

The speech used in the chapters so far has been polite speech, `존댓말`. It's better to sound too polite (a little awkward) than too casual (rude).

With close friends who are the same age as you or younger it's possible to use casual speech, `반말`.

## How to use casual speech (반말)

To make your speech casual conjugate a verb as usual without adding a `요` at the end, and without using any honorific speech or humble speech.

## Saying `I` in casual speech (반말)

In casual speech you use `나` instead of `저` for saying `I` / `me`.

A few common combinations of `나` with particles:

- 나 + 의 → 나의 → **내**
- 나 + 가 → **내가**
- 나 + 는 → **나는** or **난**
- 나 + 를 → **나를** or **날**

## How to say `you` in casual speech (반말)

With close friends who are the same age as you or younger you can either:

1. Use their name. If it ends in a consonant, attach `이`.
2. Use the word `너`. But be careful with using it, as it can sound like you are *speaking down* to someone, only use it in very close relationships.

With close friends who are older than you use the appropriate titles such as `형`, `오빠`, `누나` or `언니`.

You can always ask what they'd prefer.

`너` with common particles:

- 너 + 의 → 너의 → 네 <small>(This is more often pronounced as "니", to avoid confusion with `내` (mine))</small>
- 너 + 가 → 네가 <small>(This is more often pronounced as "니가", to avoid confusion with `내가`)</small>
- 너 + 는 → 너는 or 넌
- 너 + 를 → 너를 or 널

## `Yes` and `no` in casual speech (반말)

The words for `yes` and `no` are different in casual speech.

To say **yes** (`네`) use `응`. You might also encounter `어` to be used for the same thing.

To say **no** (`아니요`) use `아니`.

## `이다` and `아니다` in casual speech (반말)

The verbs `이다` and `아니다` have different conjugations in casual speech.

`이다` will become `이야` after a consonant, or `야` after a vowel.

`아니다` will become `아니야`.

## Dropping the object marker

In casual speech the object marker (`을` or `를`) is often dropped to help make speaking faster and easier. However, only do this when the meaning of the sentence is still clear.

`나는 요즘 한국어를 공부하고 있어.` → `나는 요즘 한국어 공부하고 있어.`

It's also possible to drop the object marker when speaking politely (`존댓말`). However, know that dropping the object marker will often make your Korean sound less formal.

## Casual commands

Instead of using the (으)세요 form you can also make commanding casual speech by conjugating the verb as usual.

**Honorific**: 빨리 하세요 → Do it quickly.  
**Honorific**: 빨리 해 주세요 → Please do it quickly.

**Informal**: 빨리 해요. → Do it quickly.  
**Informal**: 빨리 해 주세요. → Please do it quickly.

**Casual**: 빨리 해. → Do it quickly.  
**Casual**: 빨리 해 줘. → Please do it quickly.

---

:::note
Be very careful with using casual speech (반말). Remember, it's always better to sound too polite (a little awkward) than too casual (rude).
:::
