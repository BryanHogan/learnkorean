---
title: "Formal polite speech in Korean"
description: How to use and build the formal polite speech in Korean?
---

In formal situations we want to use formal polite speech. It is very respectful, so it's not used with friends or acquaintances. Formal polite speech is used in official announcements, speeches, formal letters, meeting a new boss, in the Korean army and similar situations.

Two common sentences you might have already heard that use formal polite speech:

<Sentence
    ko="생일 축하합니다."
    en="Happy birthday."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="잘 먹겠습니다."
    en="Thank you for the food. (Used before eating)"
/>

## How to use formal polite speech

The `니다` form are used for making formal polite statements.

1. Get the verb stem.
2. Depending on the ending:
    - If it ends in a consonant: Attach `습니다`.
    - If it ends in a vowel: `ㅂ니다`.
    - However, if it ends in a `ㄹ`: Remove `ㄹ`, then attach `ㅂ니다`.

For example:

- 하다 → 합니다
- 먹다 → 먹습니다
- 살다 → 삽니다

For questions you would use the `니까` form instead.

1. Get the verb stem.
2. Depending on the ending:
    - If it ends in a consonant: Attach `습니까`.
    - If it ends in a vowel: `ㅂ니까`.
    - However, if it ends in a `ㄹ`: Remove `ㄹ`, then attach `ㅂ니까`.

For example:

- 하다 → 합니까
- 먹다 → 먹습니까
- 살다 → 삽니까

For commands and suggestions you would use different endings:

- For commands: Use `(으)십시오`, e.g. 가십시오. (Please go.)
- For suggestions: Use `ㅂ시다` / `읍시다`, e.g. 갑시다. (Let's go.) Avoid this form when speaking to someone who needs more respect, as it can sound too direct. See [Making suggestions](/chapter/making-suggestions) for more details.

## Future tense with formal polite speech

There are two common ways to build future tense with formal polite speech.

One way is to change the end of the `것이다` form.

So for sentences this will become `것입니다`, commonly shortened to `겁니다`.

For questions it will become `것입니까`, commonly shortened to `겁니까`.

<Sentence
    ko="저는 갈 **겁니다**."
    en="I will go. (Formal polite speech)"
/>
<Sentence
    ko="사장님도 가실 **겁니까**?"
    en="Will you go too, boss?"
/>

The other way is to use `겠다`. The future tense `겠다` form sounds more polite than the future tense `것이다` form.

`Verb Stem + 겠다`

1. Get the verb stem.
2. Attach `겠다` and conjugate it. So either `겠습니다` or `겠습니까` in formal polite speech.

<Sentence
    ko="제가 하겠습니다."
    en="I will do it. (Formal polite speech)"
/>
<Sentence
    ko="언제 나가겠습니까?"
    en="When will you leave?"
/>

## Past tense with formal polite speech

To make the past tense form of formal polite speech you also use `습니다` / `습니까`.

Get the past tense verb stem and attach `습니다` / `습니까`.

<Sentence
    ko="제가 **했습니다**."
    en="I did it. (Formal polite speech)"
/>
<Sentence
    ko="사장님이 **하셨습니까**?"
    en="Did you do it, boss?"
/>
