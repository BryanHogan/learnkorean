An introduction to reading Hangul and understanding the letters of the Korean alphabet.

## Introduction

Learning to read Hangul does not take long. Hangul has 24 basic letters: 14 consonants and 10 vowels. It also has 16 compound consonants and vowels, which we will cover later.

Hangul is made up of consonants, such as `ㄱ` (g) and `ㅁ` (m), and vowels, such as `ㅏ` (a) and `ㅜ` (u). The letters are grouped into syllable blocks. Each written block contains at least one consonant and one vowel.

The consonant `ㅇ` has no sound when it fills the initial position. For example, `ㅇ` and `ㅏ` (a) combine to create `아` (a).

This allows us to create sounds such as:

|  | ㅏ | ㅜ | 
| --- | --- | --- | 
| ㅇ | 아 (a) | 우 (u) | 
| ㄱ | 가 (ga) |  구 (gu) | 
| ㅁ | 마 (ma) | 무 (mu) | 

<Aside>Romanization can help you recognize new letters, but it cannot represent Korean pronunciation perfectly. Focus on learning Hangul rather than relying on Roman letters.</Aside>


## Basic Consonants
The ten consonants introduced in this lesson are: `ㄱ`, `ㄴ`, `ㄷ`, `ㄹ`, `ㅁ`, `ㅂ`, `ㅅ`, `ㅇ`, `ㅈ`, `ㅎ`

- `ㄱ` = g/k
- `ㄴ` = n
- `ㄷ` = d/t
- `ㄹ` = r/l
- `ㅁ` = m
- `ㅂ` = b/p
- `ㅅ` = s
- `ㅇ` = silent initially; ng finally
- `ㅈ` = j
- `ㅎ` = h

You will need to memorize these letters. You can practise them with this [pre-made Hangul Anki deck](/files/ToLearnKoreanHangul.apkg).

These English sounds are only rough comparisons:

- `ㄱ` sounds like a mix between "g" and "k".
- `ㄴ` is similar to an "n" sound.
- `ㄷ` sounds like a mix between "d" and "t".
- `ㄹ` can sound like an "r" or "l", depending on its position.
- `ㅁ` is similar to an "m" sound.
- `ㅂ` sounds like a mix between "b" and "p".
- `ㅅ` is similar to an "s" sound.
- `ㅇ` is silent at the beginning of a syllable and represents an "ng" sound at the end.
- `ㅈ` sounds closer to "j" than "ch". The aspirated "ch" sound is represented by `ㅊ`, which we will cover later.
- `ㅎ` is similar to an "h" sound.

Reading and memorizing these letters can be difficult at first. It is normal to read very slowly as a beginner. You will become faster with practice.

The letter `ㄹ` can be difficult for Korean learners because it does not exactly match the English "r" or "l". It is usually romanized as r before a vowel and l before a consonant or at the end of a word. [This video explains the pronunciation](https://youtu.be/2-4ShB33Eu4), but do not worry about sounding perfect when you are starting out.

## Basic Vowels

Next, let's focus on these six vowels: `ㅏ`, `ㅓ`, `ㅗ`, `ㅜ`, `ㅡ`, `ㅣ`
- `ㅏ` = a
- `ㅓ` = eo (sounds roughly like "uh")
- `ㅗ` = o
- `ㅜ` = u
- `ㅡ` = eu
- `ㅣ` = i

Combined with `ㄱ`, they form: 가 (ga), 거 (geo), 고 (go), 구 (gu), 그 (geu), 기 (gi).

Combined with `ㄷ`, they form: 다 (da), 더 (deo), 도 (do), 두 (du), 드 (deu), 디 (di).

## Building Syllable Blocks

Each written Korean syllable block contains at least one consonant and one vowel.

|    | ㄱ | ㄴ | ㄷ | ㄹ | ㅁ | ㅂ | ㅅ | ㅇ | ㅈ | ㅎ |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| ㅏ | 가     | 나     | 다     | 라     | 마     | 바     | 사     | 아     | 자     | 하     |
| ㅓ | 거     | 너     | 더     | 러     | 머     | 버     | 서     | 어     | 저     | 허     |
| ㅗ | 고     | 노     | 도     | 로     | 모     | 보     | 소     | 오     | 조     | 호     |
| ㅜ | 구     | 누     | 두     | 루     | 무     | 부     | 수     | 우     | 주     | 후     |
| ㅡ | 그     | 느     | 드     | 르     | 므     | 브     | 스     | 으     | 즈     | 흐     |
| ㅣ | 기     | 니     | 디     | 리     | 미     | 비     | 시     | 이     | 지     | 히     |

*Pronunciation note: 시 is romanized as si, but its pronunciation is closer to the English word "she".*

Blocks with one consonant and one vowel use one of two layouts: side by side or top to bottom.

![How to build syllable blocks simple](../assets/images/BlockBuilding-ToLearnKorean.com.png)

In the image, C means consonant and V means vowel.

Vowels with a vertical main stroke, such as `ㅏ`, `ㅓ`, and `ㅣ`, are placed to the right of the initial consonant. Vowels with a horizontal main stroke, such as `ㅗ`, `ㅜ`, and `ㅡ`, are placed below it.

Words in Korean can look like this:  
- 아기 (agi): baby
- 고기 (gogi): meat
- 가방 (gabang): backpack
- 사람 (saram): person
- 물 (mul): water
- 손가락 (songarak): finger

A syllable block can also include a final consonant. A consonant at the bottom of a syllable block is called 받침 (batchim).

![How to build syllable blocks with a final consonant](../assets/images/BlockBuilding2-ToLearnKorean.com.png)

The numbers in the image show the reading order: initial consonant, vowel, and then final consonant.

In the word examples we can also see how the character `ㅇ` works.  At the beginning of a syllable, `ㅇ` is silent. In 아기 (agi), it fills the initial position so that `ㅏ` can be written as part of a syllable block. At the end of a syllable, `ㅇ` represents an "ng" sound, as in 가방 (gabang).

In 응급 (eunggeup), the first `ㅇ` is silent and the second represents "ng". The word means emergency.


That is almost everything you need to know about building these syllable blocks. Some blocks contain four letters, including two final consonants. We will cover their pronunciation in a later lesson.

In the next lesson, we will cover the remaining consonants and vowels. Luckily, they are similar to the ones you already know.
