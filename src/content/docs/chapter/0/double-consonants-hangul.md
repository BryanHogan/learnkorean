Korean has five double consonants: `ㄲ`, `ㄸ`, `ㅃ`, `ㅆ`, and `ㅉ`.

## Writing double consonants

Each double consonant fits inside one syllable block, written twice but still within the same space:

- `ㄱ` → `ㄲ`
- `ㄷ` → `ㄸ`
- `ㅂ` → `ㅃ`
- `ㅅ` → `ㅆ`
- `ㅈ` → `ㅉ`

## Pronunciation

Double consonants use the same general mouth position as their plain counterparts, but they begin more abruptly, slightly tensing your mouth before pronouncing them. Briefly hold back the airflow and release the sound with very little puff of air.

They are also commonly called **tense consonants** hence.

- ㄲ (Tense K): Sounds close to a hard, clipped “g” to English speakers, whereas ㄱ is a softer, breathier mix of “g/k.” To pronounce ㄲ correctly, mimic the crisp, airless “k” in the English word sky.
- ㄸ (Tense T): Sounds close to a hard, clipped “d,” whereas ㄷ is a softer mix of “d/t.” To pronounce ㄸ correctly, mimic the sharp, airless “t” in the English word stop.
- ㅃ (Tense P): Sounds close to a hard, clipped “b,” whereas ㅂ is a softer mix of “b/p.” To pronounce ㅃ correctly, mimic the tight, airless “p” in the English word spy.
- ㅆ (Tense S): A sharper, tighter, and more forceful “s” than the regular ㅅ. Because it is voiceless, it should never vibrate like a “z.”
- ㅉ (Tense J/Ch): A sharper, more abrupt, and higher-tension version of ㅈ. While officially romanized as “jj,” think of it as a “ch/j” sound where you build up pressure behind your teeth and release it with extra force but no air.

## Plain, aspirated, and tense consonants

We now know these three related types of consonants:

- **Plain consonants** begin with a relatively gentle release.
- **Aspirated (strong) consonants** release a noticeable puff of air.
- **Tense (double) consonants** begin abruptly with very little puff of air.

| Plain | Aspirated | Tense |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ㄱ (g/k) | ㅋ (k) | ㄲ (kk) |
| ㄷ (d/t) | ㅌ (t) | ㄸ (tt) |
| ㅂ (b/p) | ㅍ (p) | ㅃ (pp) |
| ㅈ (j) | ㅊ (ch) | ㅉ (jj) |
| ㅅ (s) | — | ㅆ (ss) |

Compare these syllables:

| Plain | Aspirated | Tense |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 가 | 카 | 까 |
| 다 | 타 | 따 |
| 바 | 파 | 빠 |
| 자 | 차 | 짜 |
| 사 | — | 싸 |

## Word examples

These common words begin with tense consonants:

- 꿈 = dream
- 딸 = daughter
- 빵 = bread
- 쓰다 = to write; to use
- 짜다 = to be salty

Try practising these with actual words in which they come up, that should feel easier and more natural than listening to these sounds in isolation.
