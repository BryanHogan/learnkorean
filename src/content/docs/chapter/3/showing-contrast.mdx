title: "Using 데 for showing contrast"
description: How to show contrast using the 데 form and how it's different from 지만.
---

The `지만` and `데` form can be used to show contrast. This lesson will focus on using `데` for contrast.

We already learned the `지만`, meaning `but`, in a previous lesson. For example: I also want to hang out but I have no time. → 저도 같이 놀고 싶지만 시간이 없어요.

`지만` shows strong contrast, it shows there's something different between the first and second part of the sentence.

A common way to show contrast also is using `은` / `는`, the topic marker. It can show contrast by itself without additional grammar. You can think of it as meaning something like "As for ..." or "When it comes to ...".

## Showing contrast using `데`

The `데` form is also used to show contrast, but it indicates less contrast than the `지만` form. `데` can translate to both `but` and `and`.

### 데 with action verbs

`Action Verb Stem + 는데`

1. Get the verb stem of an action verb.
2. Attach `는데`.
    - For action verb stems that end with the consonant `ㄹ`, remove it and then attach the `는데`.
3. Finish the rest of the sentence, or attach 요 if it's used in the end of a sentence.

For example:

- 좋아하다 → 좋아하는데
- 먹다 → 먹는데
- 살다 → 사는데
- 있다 → 있는데
- 없다 → 없는데

`있다` and `없다` use this approach as well, even when used as descriptive verbs. This also applies to adjectives ending in them: 맛있다 → 맛있는데.

### 데 with descriptive verbs

`Descriptive verb stem + (은/ㄴ) 데`

1. Get the verb stem of a descriptive verb.
2. Depending on the ending:
    - Ends in a consonant: Attach `은데`.
    - Ends in a vowel: attach `ㄴ데`.
    - Ends with the consonant `ㄹ`: Remove `ㄹ`, then attach `ㄴ데`.
    - Irregular descriptive verbs ending with the consonant `ㅂ`: Remove `ㅂ`, then attach `우` and then `ㄴ데`.
3. Finish the rest of the sentence, or attach `요` if it's used at the end of a sentence.

For example:

- 친절하다 → 친절한데
- 좋다 → 좋은데
- 길다 → 긴데
- 맵다 → 매운데

## Past tense with 데

For both action and descriptive verbs, attach `는데` after the past-tense marker (`았` / `었`):

- 가다 → 갔는데
- 먹다 → 먹었는데
- 좋다 → 좋았는데

## Future tense with 데

The future tense form `것이다` can combine with the `데` form to become `것인데`, commonly shortened to `건데`.

Example sentences:

- I will go see a movie tomorrow. → 내일 영화 보러 갈 건데요.
- I'm going to see a movie tomorrow, but my friend can't come. → 내일 영화를 보러 갈 건데 친구는 같이 못 가요.

## Example sentences using 데

It can be a bit difficult to translate this form directly into English, so let's cover some examples:

- It's raining but I don't have an umbrella. → 비가 오고 있는데 우산이 없어요.
- It snowed in the morning but now it's not cold. → 아침에 눈이 왔는데 지금은 안 추워요.
- I went to the hospital but could not meet the doctor. → 병원에 갔는데 의사를 만날 수 없었어요.
- You're busy but can you meet? → 바쁜데 만날 수 있어요?
- Kimchi is spicy but delicious. → 김치는 매운데 맛있어요.

## 데 at the end of a sentence

You can also end with `는데요` / `(으)ㄴ데요` to provide background, invite a response, or imply an unstated contrast. The meaning depends on context.


## When to use 데 or 지만

Both the `데` form and `지만` forms can translate to `but`, but there are situations where `데` will sound more natural. The `데` form is used more often when trying to explain something, e.g. English sentences which might use a `so`, `you see` or `I'm trying to explain` or similar.
