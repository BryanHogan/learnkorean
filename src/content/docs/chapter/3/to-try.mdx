---
title: "To Try Something in Korean"
description: How to say "to try doing something" in Korean using 보다, 본 적(이) 있다, and (으)려고 하다 to express trying, having tried, or intending to do something.
---

This lesson covers how to say "to try" something with action verbs, e.g. "to try eating something."

To say `"to try"` we use the action verb `보다` (to see). Here, `"try"` means giving an action a try, so it does not focus on actually succeeding at the action, but on doing it and seeing how it goes.

## Saying `to try` using 보다

1. Conjugate an action verb as usual without attaching the `요` to the end.
2. Add `보다` and conjugate it.

This form would literally translate to "to do and see".

<Sentence
    ko="김치를 먹어 **봤어요**."
    en="I tried eating kimchi."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="반찬을 만들어 **보세요**."
    en="Try making side dishes."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="한국어로 말해 **보세요**."
    en="Try saying it in Korean."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="두 번 먹어 **봤어요**."
    en="I tried eating it twice."
/>

Another way to give advice, such as telling someone to try something, is using the `should` / `should not` form covered in the [next chapter using the `(으)면` form with `좋다`](/chapter/should-and-shouldnt).

## Asking `"Have you ever?"`

To ask if someone has ever done something you can also use the `보다` form:

`Action verb stem conjugated + 본 적(이) 있다`

1. Conjugate an action verb as usual, without attaching `요` to the end.
2. Add `본 적(이) 있다` (or `없다`) and conjugate `있다`.

This form is commonly used to say that you have (or don't have) the experience of doing something. If you instead want to say that you just tried something, without emphasizing that it was an experience, use the `보다` form from the beginning instead.

`본` comes from `보다`. `적` means a `time` or `experience`. So it literally means to have, or not to have, the experience of trying to do something.

<Sentence
    ko="한국에 **가 본 적이 있어요**?"
    en="Have you ever been to Korea before?"
/>
<Sentence
    ko="그걸 **해 본 적이 없어요**."
    en="I have never tried doing that before."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="떡을 **먹어 본 적이 있어요**?"
    en="Have you ever eaten rice cake before?"
/>

## Saying `to try` using the (으)려고 form

The `(으)려고` form is used to say you are **intending** to do something, so saying that you are **going to do** or **try to do** something.

`Action verb stem + (으)려고 하다`

1. Get an action verb stem.
2. Attach `으려고` if ends in a consonant, or attach `려고` if it ends in a vowel.
    - If it ends in a `ㄹ` attach `려고` instead.
3. Add `하다` and conjugate it.

They can also be used without the `하다` at the end, as is common in conversations. So it can be omitted just like in the [quoting forms](/chapter/quotes).

It does not mean that you are successfully doing something, just like the `보다` form.

This `(으)려고` form can only be sed in 1st or 2nd person. So you can say `"I intend to..."` or `"You intend to..."`, but **not** `"He/she/someone else intends to..."`(3rd person). Just like the `고 싶다` form for wanting something which can also only be used for 1st and 2nd person.

<Sentence
    ko="집으로 돌아가려고 해요."
    en="I'm trying to go back home. / I'm intending to go back home. / I'm going to go back home."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="지금 아침을 먹으려고 하고 있어요."
    en="I'm trying to eat breakfast now. / I'm intending to eat breakfast now. / I'm going to eat breakfast now."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="한 시간 후에 돌아오려고 해요."
    en="I'll try to come back after 1 hour. / I intend to come back after 1 hour. / I'm going to come back after 1 hour."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="저는 하루 종일 열심히 자려고 하고 있었어요."
    en="I was trying to sleep all day."
/>

The `(으)려고` form can also be used to say `in order to`.

<Sentence
    ko="한국에 가려고 한국어를 배웠어요."
    en="I learned Korean in order to go to Korea. / I learned Korean, intending to go to Korea."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="저는 살을 빼려고 운동하고 있어요."
    en="I'm exercising in order to lose weight. / I'm exercising, intending to lose weight."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="차를 사려고 모으고 있어요."
    en="I'm saving up money in order to buy a car."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="그 문장을 이해하려고 선생님한테 물어봤고요."
    en="I asked the teacher in order to understand that sentence."
/>
