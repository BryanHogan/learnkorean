This lesson covers how to say "to try" something for action verbs, e.g. "I'm trying to do something.".

To say to try we use the action verb `보다` (to see). 

## Saying `to try` using 보다

1. Conjugate an action verb as usual without attaching the `요` to the end.
2. Add `보다` and conjugate it.

This form would literally translate to "to do and see".

<Sentence
    ko="김치를 먹어 **봤어요**."
    en="I tried eating kimchi."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="반찬을 만들어 **보세요**."
    en="Try making side dishes."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="한국어로 말해 **보세요**."
    en="Try saying it in Korean."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="두 번 먹어 **봤어요**."
    en="I tried eating it twice."
/>

Another way to tell someone to try something is using the `should` / `should not` form covered in the [next chapter using the `(으)면` form with `좋다`](/chapter/should-and-shouldnt).

## Asking `"Have you ever?"`

To ask if someone has ever done something you can also use the `보다` form:

`Action verb stem conjugated 본 적(이) 있다`

1. Conjugate an action verb as usual, without attaching `요` to the end.
2. Add `본 적(이) 있다` (or `없다`) and conjugate `있다`.

This form is commonly used to say that you have (or don't have) the experience of doing something. If you instead want to say that you just tried something, without emphasizing that it was an experience, use the `보다` form from the beginning instead.

`본` comes from `보다`. `적` means a `time` or `experience`. So it literally means to have, or not to have, the experience of trying to do something.

<Sentence
    ko="한국에 **가 본 적이 있어요**?"
    en="Have you ever been to Korea before?"
/>
<Sentence
    ko="그걸 **해 본 적이 없어요**."
    en="I have never tried doing that before."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="떡을 **먹어 본 적이 있어요**?"
    en="Have you ever eaten rice cake before?"
/>
