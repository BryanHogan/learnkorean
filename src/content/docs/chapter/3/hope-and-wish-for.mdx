---
title: "To hope or to wish for in Korean"
description: How to say you hope or wish for something in Korean?
---

We already learned how to say `to want to have something` using `가지다` together with the `고 싶다` form.

<Sentence
    ko="저는 새로운 자동차를 가지고 싶어요."
    en="I want a new car. / I want to have a new car."
/>

But there are more ways to express that you want or wish for something.

## `To want` using `원하다`

Another way to say `to want` in Korean can be done using the action verb `원하다`. `원하다` sounds much stranger than `가지고 싶다`, therefore it's much less common when saying you `want` something.

## To `hope for` using `바라다`

To say you `hope` something use `바라다` (to hope) with the `기` form. Since `바라다` can take an object, you can add the object marker after the `기` form. You can also shorten `기를` to `길`, as in the examples below. This `바라다` form is more commonly used in writing or formal speaking (compared to another form covered later in this chapter).

<Sentence
    ko="다시 만나**기를 바라요**."
    en="I hope we meet again."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="너무 바쁘지 않**길 바라요**."
    en="I hope you are not too busy."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="건강하고 행복하**길 바라요**."
    en="I hope you are healthy and happy."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="잘되**길 바라요**."
    en="I hope it goes well."
/>

## `Would be` or `must be` using the `겠다` form

The `겠다` form can also be used to mean `would be ` or `must be` when used together with descriptive verbs.

We know this form from the earlier [chapter about formal polite speech](/chapter/formal-polite-speech).

Here it will most often conjugate as `겠어요`. (Or `겠어` in casual speech.)

<Sentence
    ko="맛있겠어요."
    en="It would/must be delicious! / It will be delicious!"
/>
<Sentence
    ko="정말 좋겠어요."
    en="It would/must be really good. / It will be really good"
/>
<Sentence
    ko="비싸겠어요."
    en="It would/must be expensive. / It will be expensive."
/>

This form can also be used with a past tense verb stem to mean "`would have been`" or "`must have been`".

<Sentence
    ko="힘들었겠어요."
    en="That would/must have been difficult."
/>

## To `hope for` or `wish for` using `(으)면 좋겠다`

This is another way to say you `hope` or `wish` for something. This `(으)면 좋겠다` form is more commonly used when speaking than the `바라다` form.

`Verb Stem + (으)면 좋겠다`

1. Conjugate the `(으)면` form normally, including any exceptions.
2. Add `좋겠다` and conjugate it.

`좋겠다` literally means "`will be good`", "`would be good`", or "`must be good`".

You can think of this form meaning "`It would be good if...`". A natural translation for this form is "`I hope...`" or "`I wish...`".

The past-tense form `-았/었으면 좋겠다` can also express a present or future wish, often with a more hypothetical feeling, as in the weather example below.

<Sentence
    ko="주말에 비가 오**면 좋겠어요**."
    en="I hope it rains on the weekend."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="날씨가 더 좋았으**면 좋겠어요**."
    en="I wish the weather were better."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="이안 씨도 도와주**면 좋겠어요**."
    en="I hope Ian will help too."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="사장님도 오시면 좋겠습니다."
    en="I hope that you will come too, boss."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="제가 가수가 될 수 있**으면 좋겠어요**."
    en="I hope that I can become a singer."
/>
<Sentence
    ko="새로운 냉장고랑 냉동실이 잘 되**면 좋겠어요**."
    en="I hope that the new refrigerator and freezer work well."
/>
