title: "Giving a Reason"
description: How to give a reason in Korean. Using the (으)니까,  서 and 때문 form to give a reason.
---

There are several ways to give a reason in Korean. Let's cover the three most common, `(으)니까`,  `서` and `때문` form.

## Saying `because` using `(으)니까`

`Verb Stem + (으)니까`

1. Get the verb stem.
2. Attach `으니까` if it ends in a Consonant, or attach `니까` if it ends in a vowel.
    - However, for verb stems that end with the consonant `ㄹ`, remove it and attach `니까`.  
    For example: 살다 → 사니까
    - However, for verb stems that end with the consonant `ㅂ`, remove it and attach `우`, then `니까`.  
    For example: 맵다 → 매우니까
3. Finish the rest of the sentence, or attach `요` if it's us ed at the end of a sentence.

The reason will always come before the because form (this applies to any because form).

The `(으)니까` form is not a formal sounding grammar form, although it's also not impolite. The more formal version would be xxx which is covered further below.

### Example sentences using the `(으)니까` form

- Be careful because the food is hot. → 음식이 뜨거우니까 조심하세요.
- The apple is more delicious because it is cold. → 사과가 차기우니까 더 맛있어요.
- I am always busy because I am working every day. → 저는 매일 일을 하고 있으니까 항상 바빠요.
- It's because I'm not Korean. → 제가 한국 사람 아니니까요.

### Limitations of the `(으)니까` form

Exceptions for when using `(으)니까` is not possible:

- `(으)니까` can not be used to say someone feels some sort of emotion 'because' of something. So you can not us this form to say you are happy, sad, thankful or sorry. For this you have to use the `서` form.

## Saying `because` using `서`

`Verb Stem Conjugated + 서`

1. Get the verb stem.
2. Conjugate as usual with `아` or `어`, without attaching `요` at the end.
3. Attach `서`.

Building the form examples:

- 하다: 해서
- 먹다: 먹어서
- 살다: 살아서
- 맵다: 매워서

This `서` also translate to `because`, but it shows more emphasis of a **cause and effect**. Using this `서` form emphasizes that **that something was caused by something else**.

### Example sentences using the `서` form

- I went to the hospital because my head hurt. → 머리가 아파서 병원에 갔어요.
- Yesterday I couldn't not sleep because I was scared. → 어제 무서워서 잠을 잘 수 없었어요
- Because I'm a little tired. → 조금 피곤해서요..
- Thank you for coming early. → 일짝 와 줘서 고마워요.

### Limitations of the `서` form

Exceptions for when using `서` is **not** possible:

- The `서` form can **not** be used when giving a reason for a command. Only `(으)니까` can be used for that, e.g. `오늘 날씨 좋으니까 밖에서 만나세요.` needs the `(으)니까` form.
- The `서` form can **not** be used for making a suggestion. Only `(으)니까` can be used for that, e.g. `오늘 날씨 좋으니까 밖에서 만날까요?` needs the `(으)니까` form.

## Saying `because` using `때문`

The `때문` form is more common in **writing** than in speaking. It also means `because` but sounds more straightforward and more like a written expression. Therefore it's used in formal situation or in writing.

There are several ways to use the `때문` form.

### `때문` simply at the end of a sentence

The most basic form for it:

`Noun + 때문`

This form makes the noun the reason, so `because of NOUN`.

**Example sentences**:

- It's because of that problems. → 그 문제 때문이에요.
- It's because I can't eat spicy food. → 제가 매운 음식을 먹을 수 없기 때문이에요.

### `때문` in the middle of a sentence

To use the `때문` form in th middle of a sentence attach the particle `에`:

`Noun + 때문에`

**Example sentences**:

- I also can't eat Kimchi because I can't eat spicy food. → 제가 매운 음긱을 먹을 수 없기 때문에 김치도 먹을 수 없어요.
- I am full because I ate a lot of delicious food. → 맛있는 음식을 많이 먹었기 때문에 배가 물러요.
- I bought three containers because I like this Kimchi. → 이 김치를 좋아하기 때문에 세 통을 샀어요.
- I have a lot of Korean friends because I learned Korean. → 저는 한국어를 배웠기 때문에 한국인 친구들이 많이 있어요.

### Limitations of the `때문` form

The `때문` form has the same limit as the `서` form, it **cannot** be used when giving someone a command or when making a suggestion.

When using the `때문` form after a person it means you're blaming the person for something. For example: `This is all because of dad. → 이게 다 아빠 때문이에요.`

To instead thank someone use the `덕분` form after a person. `덕분` means `thanks to`. For example: `This is all thanks to dad. → 이게 다 아빠 덕문이에요.`

## Using the past tense

The `(으)니까` and `때문` form, like many other forms, can be attached to a verb stem in the past tense.

To get the past tense verb stem, conjugate the verb into the past tense as usual, then remove `어요`. The remaining stem ends in the consonant `ㅆ`, so attach `으니까`.

- 하다 → 했어요 → 했 → 했으니까
- 먹다 → 먹었어요 → 먹었 → 먹었으니까

The past-tense marker should not normally be used with the `서` form. Instead, tense is marked on the final verb, while the time of the `서` clause is understood from context.

The important difference is:

- I went to the hospital because my head hurt. → 머리가 아파서 병원에 **갔어요**.
    - The final `갔어요` establishes the past setting.
- I went to the hospital because my head hurt. → 머리가 **아팠으니까** 병원에 갔어요.
    - The `아팠으니까` explicitly marks the reason itself as past.
- I went to the hospital because my head hurt**. → 머리가 아팠기 때문에 병원에 갔어요**.
    - Like `(으)니까`, the `때문` form can be attached to the past tense stems.
